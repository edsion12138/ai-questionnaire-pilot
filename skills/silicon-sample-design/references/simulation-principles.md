# Silicon-sample design principles

## What a useful silicon sample should contain
- heterogeneity;
- moderate construct correlations;
- realistic measurement error;
- imperfect item behavior;
- subgroup differences that are plausible but not deterministic;
- some boundary cases;
- exposure-dependent missingness if applicable.

## What it should not contain
- perfect recovery of the intended structure by construction;
- identical answers within demographic groups;
- artificially inflated reliability;
- thresholds adjusted after seeing fit indices simply to improve fit;
- respondent labels that directly encode the desired outcome.

## Interpretation
Silicon results are diagnostic. A poor result may reflect an item problem, a model problem, or a simulation assumption. Record all assumptions and flag conclusions that require human data.
